## Division — method the tutor must follow

**State requirement (4.CE.2.i)** [S]: estimate and find the quotient of a one-digit divisor and a 2- or 3-digit dividend, with and without remainders, "using strategies and algorithms, **including the standard algorithm**." 4.CE.2.j covers word problems; 4.CE.2.k covers interpreting the remainder.

**IM Grade 4 Unit 6 sequence ("Multiplying and Dividing Multi-digit Numbers")** [S]:
1. Base-ten blocks to divide
2. Base-ten diagrams to represent division
3. **Partial quotients** — decompose the dividend, divide each part, add the partial quotients

**Tutor rule:** start with partial quotients or a base-ten picture. Accept and support the standard algorithm (Virginia requires it), but never *introduce* it as "the easy way" if he hasn't learned it in class yet. Ask: "How did your teacher show you?" [? — confirm order with a workbook photo]

Worked example — 156 ÷ 4:
- Partial quotients: 4 × 30 = 120 → 156 − 120 = 36 → 4 × 9 = 36 → 36 − 36 = 0 → 30 + 9 = **39**
- Standard algorithm: 15 ÷ 4 = 3 r3 → bring down 6 → 36 ÷ 4 = 9 → **39**
- Check: 39 × 4 = 156
- Remainder in context: 157 students, 4 per van → 39 r1 → **40 vans**

## Perimeter and area (4.MG.3) [S]

- **Rectangles and squares only.** Students develop the formulas from concrete and pictorial models before using them.
- Covers: given two adjacent sides (rectangle) or one side (square); same perimeter with different area, and the reverse; word problems.
- Perimeter = distance around: add all sides, or 2 × length + 2 × width. Area = length × width, in **square units**.
- Most common error to catch: giving area in plain units ("28 ft" instead of "28 square feet").
